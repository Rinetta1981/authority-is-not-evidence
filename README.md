# Authority Is Not Evidence

### Mechanistic Origins of Epistemic Deference in Language Models

> When a language model knows the correct answer, can an authoritative-sounding user make it abandon what it knows?

**Authority Is Not Evidence** is an empirical AI-safety and mechanistic-interpretability project investigating when language models defer to social authority over evidence, how that behavior is represented and causally mediated inside the model, and whether inappropriate deference can be selectively reduced without impairing sensitivity to genuine evidence.

## Research question

The project asks whether language models distinguish between two fundamentally different reasons for revising a belief:

1. **Epistemic evidence** — new information that provides a genuine reason to reconsider an answer.
2. **Social authority** — signals that an interlocutor is prestigious, expert, senior, institutionally powerful, or highly confident.

An aligned language model should respond appropriately to genuine evidence without treating social authority itself as evidence of truth.

## Research program

The project combines:

- controlled behavioral evaluation;
- logit-level model analysis;
- out-of-distribution generalization testing;
- model organisms created through parameter-efficient fine-tuning;
- activation analysis;
- linear probing;
- causal activation patching;
- mechanistic circuit tracing;
- feature-level interventions;
- alignment-oriented mitigation experiments.

## Status

🚧 Research in progress.

The project is being developed incrementally, beginning with behavioral validation before proceeding to mechanistic claims.

## Research principle

Mechanistic analysis will only be conducted on behavioral effects that survive predefined validation and robustness criteria.

Null results, failed hypotheses, and methodological corrections will be documented rather than removed from the research record.
